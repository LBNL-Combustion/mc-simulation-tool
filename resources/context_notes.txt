Context:
The code I’m going to walk you through simulates transient heat transfer, airflow, and condensation in wood stove chimney systems. It will serve as the core physics engine or backend for the tool you’re developing.
It’s built to be modular, numerically stable, and accurate for both single-wall and triple-wall chimney segments. The output includes gas temperature, draft pressure, flow rate, and wall temperatures over time.

---------------------------------------------------------

Overview of the Architecture
The codebase is broken into loosely coupled modules:
 - chimney.py sets up the chimney geometry.
 - coupled_solver.py handles the physics per segment: flow, temperature, wall conduction.
 - adaptive_triple_wall.py and stable_triple_wall.py solve heat conduction in triple-wall segments.
 - mass_flow.py and physics.py handle pressure loss and thermophysical properties.
Everything else is just physics utilities or diagnostics.

---------------------------------------------------------

Step by Step
At runtime, the main script does the following:
 1. It builds a ChimneySystem, which is a list of ChimneySegments — vertical, horizontal, elbow, etc.
 2. Each segment knows its geometry, orientation, and whether it has a single or triple wall.
 3. At each timestep:
    - A gas temperature is injected at the base from a time-varying function.
    - We call chimney.step_all_segments(...).
    - That walks up the chimney from bottom to top.
    - For each segment, it solves:
        - Flow and pressure from buoyancy and friction
        - Gas-wall heat transfer
        - Wall temperature update
        - Draft pressure generation
    - The output is the final gas temperature, flow rate, and total draft pressure.

---------------------------------------------------------

Coupled vs. Stable vs. Single-Wall Solvers
You’ll hear three kinds of solvers in this code:
 - Coupled solver → this is the main control loop for a segment. It couples gas temperature, mass flow, and wall heat loss and iterates until everything is consistent.
 - Stable wall solver → this is used for triple-wall segments. It solves the transient conduction across inner steel, insulation, and outer shell using a Crank-Nicolson matrix method. It includes radiation and adaptive time stepping for stability.
 - Single-wall → much simpler. Just an explicit update of the wall temperature based on how much heat is lost from the gas.”

---------------------------------------------------------

What you'll be hooking into:
From a backend perspective, the most relevant function is:
chimney.step_all_segments(T_inlet, T_ambient, P_ambient)
It returns:
 - Outlet gas temperature
 - Total draft pressure
 - Flow rate (ṁ)
Internally, it takes care of solving all segments, whether single- or triple-wall, and accounts for elbows, insulation, condensation, etc.

---------------------------------------------------------

The main driver is just a loop over time:
for t in time_steps:
    T_flue = fire_profile(t)
    T_out, draft, ṁ = chimney.step_all_segments(...)
It stores outputs as lists of time series — easy to pipe into a plot or return to a web app.
